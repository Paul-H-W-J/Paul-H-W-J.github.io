Privacy policy

YT-CLI · Effective 9 October 2026 · Home

This policy covers YT-CLI, a read-only YouTube client for the terminal, and the YT-CLI sign-in service. Both are operated by Paul Ho Wei Jian. This policy says what Google user data YT-CLI accesses, how it uses that data, where the data is stored, and who the data is shared with.

1. What YT-CLI accesses

YT-CLI requests exactly two OAuth scopes:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.readonly — read-only access to your YouTube account. YT-CLI uses it to list the channels you subscribe to, and to run the searches you type.
  • openid — an opaque, stable identifier for your Google account (the sub claim). The sign-in service uses it to recognise you when you return and to apply the shared daily search limit. It is not your email address and cannot be read as one.

YT-CLI requests no other scope. It does not request your name, your email address, your profile picture, your watch history, or your watch-later list. Read-only means read-only: YT-CLI cannot upload, comment, like, rate, subscribe, unsubscribe, edit a playlist, or change any setting on your account.

2. How YT-CLI uses the data

Only to make the client work:

  • to show your subscription list and its long-form uploads in the terminal grid;
  • to run the YouTube searches you ask for and show the results;
  • to stop any one account from exhausting the daily search allowance that all users share.

Google user data is never used for advertising, never used to build a profile of you, never sold, and never used to train a machine-learning or AI model. It is not used for any purpose other than the three above.

3. Where the data is stored

YT-CLI can sign in two ways, and they store different things.

With your own Google credentials (local only)

If you supply your own OAuth client with YT-CLI login --client-secret, your machine talks to Google directly. The OAuth token is written to ~/.yt-cli/token.json on your own computer, with private file permissions. No server of ours receives your token or your YouTube data.

Through the YT-CLI sign-in service (the default)

Plain YT-CLI login uses the YT-CLI sign-in service, so that you do not have to create a Google Cloud project. The service runs on Supabase. It stores the minimum needed to act on your behalf:

  • The opaque Google account identifier described in section 1.
  • A Google refresh token, encrypted at rest. This is what lets the service read your subscription list and run searches without asking you to sign in again.
  • A session record. Your terminal receives a random session token; the service stores only a one-way hash of it, with the times it was created and last used.
  • A daily search count. One number per account per day. The search terms themselves are not stored.
  • Server and rate-limit records. The hosting platform may log a request with an IP address and a timestamp. For rate limiting, the service converts the address at once into a keyed, one-way identifier; that identifier, not the address, may remain for up to one day.

Either way, your subscription list is cached on your own computer, and the uploads grid is fetched by your own computer straight from YouTube's public RSS feeds. Which videos you open is never sent to us.

4. What is never stored

  • Which videos you watch.
  • Your name, email address, or profile picture.
  • Your search terms, once the search returns.
  • Your subscription list, once it reaches your computer.
  • Your Google password. Sign-in happens only on Google's own pages.

5. Who the data is shared with

Nobody. Google user data obtained through YT-CLI is not sold, not rented, not transferred, and not shared with any third party. The only outbound requests YT-CLI makes with your data are to Google's own APIs, on your behalf. The one exception is disclosure compelled by law.

YT-CLI uses Supabase purely as the hosting platform for the sign-in service. Supabase processes the stored records on our instructions as a service provider; it is not given the data for its own use.

6. Google API Services User Data Policy

YT-CLI's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

YT-CLI is a YouTube API Services client. By using it you are also bound by the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's own handling of your data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.

7. Revoking access, retention, and deletion

You can revoke YT-CLI's access to your Google account at any time at https://myaccount.google.com/permissions. Revoking makes any stored token useless immediately.

To revoke access and delete your stored record in one step, run:

YT-CLI logout --forget-account

Your account record and encrypted token are kept until you delete them. Daily search counts are discarded after 30 days. Unfinished sign-in attempts are discarded within minutes. If you cannot run the command above, write to paulhoweijian@gmail.com and your record will be deleted.

8. Security

Refresh tokens are encrypted at rest. Session tokens and sign-in codes are stored only as one-way hashes, so a copy of the database yields no usable credential. All traffic to the service uses HTTPS. On your own computer, ~/.yt-cli/ is created with private file permissions. No system is perfectly secure, and no guarantee of absolute security is offered.

9. Children

YT-CLI is not directed at children under 13, and accounts for children are not knowingly served.
